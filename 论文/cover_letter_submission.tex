\noindent
Please consider our manuscript, ``Field accuracy is not heat accuracy:
wall-flux-constrained surrogates for non-Boussinesq convection'', for publication in the
\emph{International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer} as a full-length article.

Surrogates for thermal convection are usually trained on paired Boussinesq and compressible
solutions. Public paired data sets fix the relative temperature contrast, so a network trained
on them memorises one correction field and cannot rescale it at another contrast. The
manuscript asks whether the metric used to select these surrogates is the right one. On 200
held-out random-wall samples, a model with a 0.5\% median temperature-field error misses the
both-wall integral Nusselt number by 4.0\%; in 66\% of the samples the field error is below 1\%
and the $Nu$ error above 2\%. The same decoupling holds on the classical-cavity samples
(0.09\% field error, 1.4--2.9\% $Nu$ error across seeds). A wall-flux penalty in the training
loss reduces the integral error below 1\% and the local wall-flux error to about 1\% or less, with no
loss of field accuracy.

The underlying correction factorises as $D=\varepsilon P(X)+O(\varepsilon^2)$ with
$\varepsilon=\Delta T/T_0$. Across 627 paired solutions, the velocity correction follows
$R=A\varepsilon(1+\kappa\varepsilon)$ with exponents indistinguishable from one; the prefactor
is a functional of the flow. Training a network on $P=D/\varepsilon$ and multiplying by
$\varepsilon$ outside makes a public single-contrast data set reusable within its
boundary-condition family, reducing the channel-averaged extrapolation error from 0.131 to
0.085. A plain model trained at the same contrast and rescaled by $\varepsilon/\varepsilon_0$
performs within a few per cent, so most of the gain comes from the scaling law rather than a new
network architecture.

The study is within the journal's scope: it quantifies the departure from the
Oberbeck--Boussinesq approximation, tests it on independently generated public data, and
provides a surrogate design that preserves wall heat transfer. The main sweep is
two-dimensional and steady; a 33-sample three-dimensional check supports the exponent, and the
remaining scope is stated explicitly. The 256$^2$ Boussinesq solution is within 0.37\% of the
Le Qu\'er\'e benchmark at $Ra=10^6$. The work is original, is not under consideration
elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission. The authors declare no competing
interests. The public data are cited, and our generated data, solver configuration and
reproduction scripts are available at the repository listed in the manuscript.
